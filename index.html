See it in action

Not just what to spell. Why it's spelled that way.

Here's what Scout does with a list like this one. Each pattern gets a plain-language rule and a memory trick, so the learning carries over to words that were never on the list.

This week's list
lightnightbright raintrain playstay
igh says the letter i

The ruleThe gh is silent. It just sits there!

Memory trick“The light in the night is bright.”

Now they can spellhigh · sigh · fight

ai says the letter a

The ruleai hides in the middle of a word.

Memory trick“ai hides in the middle. Rain, train, paint.”

Now they can spellwait · snail · paint

ay says the letter a too

The ruleay likes to play at the end of a word.

Memory trick“Day, play, stay. Ay goes away, at the end.”

Now they can spellday · tray · away

Why this matters: rain and play make the same sound, so memorizing alone leaves a child guessing. Knowing that ai sits in the middle and ay sits at the end means they can spell snail and away correctly, even though neither was on this week's list.

The quest

Seven sprints, one confident speller.

Each sprint practices the same words a different way, from hearing them for the first time to spelling them from memory. Scout turns them into daily sprints across the nights you have, so a short week never skips a sprint.
